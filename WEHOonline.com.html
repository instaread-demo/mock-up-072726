Truck Bursts Into Flames on Formosa Avenue, Fire Crews Forced to Let It Burn

Wednesday - July 22, 2026 by Brian Holt | 1 Comment

Instaread

Fire crews responded to a truck fire at Santa Monica Boulevard and Formosa Avenue Wednesday afternoon.

The fire broke out in the 1100 block of Santa Monica Boulevard. Flames engulfed at least half of the truck, according to a witness at the scene.

A worker was filling a tank connected to a pressure washer at the time, according to witnesses. The tank ignited. The man suffered a burn to his hand and face.

He was asked if the fire involved gasoline from a generator. “I’m guessing it is,” he said.

A resident who lives nearby said he saw the same man spraying the ground beforehand. He said he assumed the man was cleaning the street and walked away from the scene. At some point later he saw the truck in flames and assumed the man would try to put the fire out himself but soon realized the man didn’t have the right equipment for it. He then realized it was something water alone couldn’t handle. It was then he said the fire appeared to involve at least half of the truck and called 911.

He said the 911 operator transferred him to West Hollywood emergency dispatch after he gave his address, and the call dropped before he could finish reporting the fire. He said it felt like it was awhile before he heard sirens, and the response didn’t feel fast enough.

Firefighters worked to put the fire out but were struggling to get it under control. A Los Angeles County Fire Department captain on scene said the fuel was burning as it ran down the street, and water was not stopping it.

He said fuel continued to drip from underneath the truck’s fuel tank, feeding the flames. He said crews had to stop applying water because it was carrying burning fuel further down the street instead of extinguishing it. He said the fire might have to burn out on its own.

Smoke and fumes from the fire spread into nearby apartment buildings. James, who lives near the scene told WEHOonline he was walking home from Thunderroad Cafe when he saw the fire truck heading toward his street.

He said the truck that was on fire appeared to be exploding, with flames rising and popping sounds coming from the truck. He said he remembered his own windows were open at his apartment and ran back to close them but it was too late, smoke had already gotten in his apartment by the time he got there.

“It smells like awful chemicals,” James said.

Officials closed Formosa Avenue in both directions near the intersection. Residents attempting to reach nearby buildings were turned away. Juliana, a resident at 1122 Formosa Avenue, waited in her car near the closure. She said she was trying to reach her apartment to check on her animals. Her windows were open at the time of the fire. This story is developing. WEHOonline will update as more information becomes available.
